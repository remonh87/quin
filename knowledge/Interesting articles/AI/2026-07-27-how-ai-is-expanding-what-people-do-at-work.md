# How AI is expanding what people do at work

**Date:** 2026-07-27
**URL:** https://openai.com/index/how-ai-is-expanding-what-people-do-at-work
**Verdict:** MEDIUM

- **AI-driven task crossover reshapes roles**: 43.5% of occupation-specific AI use involves tasks outside the user’s primary role (e.g., 77% for customer experience workers, 75% for designers). For engineers, 18.5% of messages involve cross-occupation tasks, while engineering tasks appear in 7.4% of messages from other fields—highlighting AI’s role in redistributing technical work.

- **Marketing and engineering tasks dominate cross-field influence**: Marketing tasks spread widely (e.g., creating materials appears in 5 other fields), while engineering tasks like troubleshooting software frequently migrate to non-engineers. This suggests AI blurs traditional boundaries, enabling non-specialists to handle technical work.

- **Small businesses see higher crossover**: Workers in small teams (2–5 seats) use AI for 18.9% cross-occupation tasks vs. 16.3% in large firms (>100 seats), implying AI acts as a "generalist tool" where specialists are scarce—critical for developers in resource-constrained environments.

**Verdict**: **MEDIUM** for a busy software developer focused on AI-assisted engineering.
*Why?* While the study reveals macro trends in AI-driven task redistribution—including engineering-adjacent crossover—it lacks granularity on software-specific workflows (e.g., coding, debugging, DevOps). The insights are too broad to directly inform AI tooling decisions in development. Prioritize developer-focused AI research (e.g., GitHub Copilot studies) instead.
